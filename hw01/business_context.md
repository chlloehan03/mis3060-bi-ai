# Business Context
## Question 1
### Prompt
Our portfolio includes borrowers with a range of credit scores, debt-to-income ratios, and annual incomes across five loan purposes (Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business). How might the importance of these borrower attributes differ across the different loan purposes?
### Summary of Claude's Response
Credit score tends to matter most where the loan is unsecured or only weakly secured, since it's the lender's main window into repayment history when there's no asset to fall back on. DTI is really a measure of how much room a borrower has to absorb a new payment, so its importance scales with how large the new obligation is relative to the borrower's existing commitments.Put together, the general pattern is that unsecured, income-dependent purposes (Personal, Business) lean more heavily on DTI and income, credit score dominates where there's little or no collateral and repayment history is the best available signal (Personal, Education), and collateral-backed purposes (Auto, and Home Improvement when secured) show comparatively flatter importance across all three, since the lender's risk is partly hedged by the underlying asset regardless of borrower financials.

## Question 2
### Prompt
Considering Wildcat's loan-purpose mix (Auto, Home Improvement, Personal, Education, Business), what would a portfolio committee typically want to see in a portfolio that is similar to this one, and would certain loan purposes get monitored differently?
### Summary of Claude's Response
A portfolio committee looking at a mix like Wildcat's would generally want three layers of visibility: the composition of the book, the credit quality trend within it, and how each segment is performing against expectations and limits — with loan purpose as one of the key cuts they'd slice all of that by. On composition, they'd want to see the dollar and account mix across the five purposes and how it's shifted over recent quarters, since a portfolio committee cares as much about drift as about the current snapshot — if Business or Personal lending has been growing faster than Auto, for example, that changes the portfolio's overall risk and return profile even if each segment looks fine in isolation. On credit quality, they'd want trend lines on the same attributes you're already tracking — average credit score, DTI, and income — by loan purpose and by vintage (the quarter or year each loan was originated), because a portfolio can look healthy on average while newer vintages are quietly underwriting to weaker standards. So the committee's core ask is usually the same shared dashboard — mix, quality trend, delinquency/loss, reserves — cut by purpose, but the deeper monitoring underneath differs because "risk" means something different in a secured, short-duration Auto loan than it does in a long-horizon, unsecured Education loan.

## Question 3
### Prompt
When looking at Wildcat's four loan status categories, how can we recognize the difference between a delinquency and a default?
### Summary of Claude's Response
Delinquent means the borrower has fallen behind on scheduled payments, but the loan is still considered an active, potentially recoverable obligation. Default, by contrast, is a classification decision: the lender has determined the loan is no longer performing as agreed and treats it as impaired, typically after delinquency has persisted past some threshold. So every default loan will almost always have passed through delinquency first, but not every delinquent loan becomes a default — many cure. Looking at your actual file (1,454 Current, 532 Paid Off, 248 Delinquent, 106 Default), the categories do show a sensible credit-quality gradient: average credit score runs 685 for Current, 670 for Paid Off, 646 for Delinquent, and 617 for Default — so Default loans carry meaningfully weaker credit profiles than Delinquent ones, which is a reasonable sanity check that the status labels track real risk. DTI is nearly flat across all four groups (0.31–0.33), so it doesn't cleanly separate delinquent from default in this dataset.
